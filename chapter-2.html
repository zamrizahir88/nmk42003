Chapter 2

Embedded Controller

By the End of This Chapter You Can

  • describe the ESP32's main features, its low-power modes and what each of its pins can do
  • choose a suitable power supply and program the board by cable, with the buttons, or over Wi-Fi
  • use digital and analog inputs and outputs, the DAC and PWM
  • explain and compare UART, SPI, I²C, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, and pick the right one for a job

Everything below is hands-on: a pinout explorer, a programming simulator and a virtual I/O lab. Tap Show on any panel to see the step-by-step working. The exercises at the end check what you have learnt.

Overview

The ESP32 is a complete measuring and communicating computer on one small board. Start with what it is, what it can do and how its pins are arranged.

    Power Supply Setup

    The board needs a steady supply. Choose the right one for your project, and check that it can deliver enough current.

      Programming the ESP32

      How a program gets from your PC into the ESP32: by cable, manually or automatically, or over Wi-Fi.

        Input and Output

        Digital and analog inputs and outputs, and pulse width modulation, the five experiments of the lab.

        Wired Connectivity

        Three serial protocols connect the ESP32 to sensors, displays and other boards: UART, SPI and I²C.

          Wireless Connectivity

          The ESP32 has Bluetooth and Wi-Fi built in, so it can talk to phones, PCs and cloud servers with no cables.

            Exercises

            Check what you know about the ESP32's structure, programming modes and connectivity. Choose or type your answers and press Check answers. Each wrong try unlocks a hint, and when all your answers are correct the explanation appears. Swipe left or right, or use the arrows, to move between exercises.
