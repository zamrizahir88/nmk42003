Chapter 4

Transduction Techniques

By the End of This Chapter You Can

  • explain why passive sensors need a transduction circuit, and name the three common techniques
  • calculate the output of voltage dividers and Wheatstone bridges, and the balance condition
  • calculate the frequency of a 555 timing circuit and the resonant frequency of an LC circuit
  • explain how a piezo sensor's charge is turned into a voltage, and why it can't measure a steady force

Introduction

From a change in R, C or L to a signal a microcontroller can read.

Voltage Divider

The simplest transduction circuit: a sensor in series with a fixed part turns a change of R, C or L into a voltage.

    Bridge Circuits

    Two dividers side by side remove the voltage offset. A galvanometer shows whether the bridge is balanced.

      Timing Circuit

      A 555 timer turns a capacitive sensor into a frequency.

        Tuned Circuit

        An inductor and a capacitor resonate at one frequency, which moves when the sensor changes L or C.

          Transduction Circuit for Active Sensors

          Turning a piezo sensor's charge into a voltage.

          Home Exercise: Submit on URLearn

          We will try some of these in class. Work them all out using the methods on this page, then submit your answers on URLearn after class. The answers are not given here.

          1. In a voltage divider circuit, Vin = 12 V, R1 = 4 kΩ and R2 = 4 kΩ.
            • (a) Calculate the output voltage Vout.
            • (b) Observe the effect of changing R2 on Vout: if R2 is decreased to 2 kΩ, and if R2 is increased to 8 kΩ.
            • (c) Observe the effect of changing R1 on Vout: if R1 is decreased to 2 kΩ, and if R1 is increased to 8 kΩ.
            • (d) Make a conclusion from your observations.
          2. A Wheatstone bridge has a 20 V supply and R1 = 120 kΩ, R2 = 480 kΩ, R3 = x and R4 = 60 kΩ, as shown. The bridge is balanced (Vout = 0). Find the value x of R3.
          3. In a Wheatstone bridge, R1 = 100 Ω, R2 = 200 Ω, R3 = 300 Ω and R4 = 50 Ω. A 10 V source is connected across the bridge (A positive), and a galvanometer is connected between points C and D to detect the balance condition.
            • (a) Calculate the output voltage Vout across points C and D.
            • (b) Determine the direction of the galvanometer current (from C to D, or from D to C) from your Vout.
          4. Pop quiz: a circuit has two resistors R1 = 6.0 Ω and R2 = 1.5 Ω, a variable resistor Rvar, a resistor of unknown value Ru and a 9.0 V battery, connected as shown. When Rvar is adjusted to 12 Ω, there is zero current through the ammeter. What is the unknown resistance Ru?
