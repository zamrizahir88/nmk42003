Lab 1 · Virtual lab

Introduction (Software & Hardware)

Try the whole of Lab 1 here before you come to the lab. You'll power and program the ESP32, blink its built-in LED, upload a program over Wi-Fi, and then switch LEDs from a phone in three ways: a Bluetooth terminal, your own MIT App Inventor app and a web page.

Objectives

  • power and program the ESP32, by cable and over the air (OTA)
  • switch the built-in LED on and off from a mobile phone by Bluetooth
  • build a simple phone app with MIT App Inventor
  • switch an external LED on and off over Wi-Fi from a web browser
  • read a switch and monitor its condition on the Serial Monitor, a phone and a web page

This is a simulation: nothing here talks to a real board. Everything follows the lab sheet, so in the lab you'll do the same steps on the real ESP32. Where the page asks for your name, it uses it the way the lab sheet does (in the Bluetooth name and the Serial Monitor).

🔒 The Lab Task unlocks when you pass the Pre-Lab Check quiz with at least 7 out of 10.

Before You Start

The equipment, the software and the basic ESP32 circuit.

    Lesson 1: Blink the Built-in LED

    Build the blocks in TUNIOT, then upload the code in two ways: by USB cable and over Wi-Fi.

      Lesson 2: Control the LED with a Bluetooth Terminal

      The ESP32 becomes a Bluetooth device with your name. You type ON or OFF on your phone.

        Lesson 3: Control the LED with Your Own App

        Design a phone app with MIT App Inventor that sends ON and OFF to the same ESP32 program.

          Lesson 4: Control an External LED over Wi-Fi

          Wire an LED to GPIO32, and let the ESP32 serve a web page with ON and OFF buttons.

            Lesson 5: Monitor the Switch Condition

            Add a toggle switch, read its condition, and monitor it in three ways: on the Serial Monitor, on a phone via Bluetooth and on a web page.
