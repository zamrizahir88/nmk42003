Amplifiers

An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a differential voltage amplifier with two inputs and one output. It has very high gain, very high input impedance and very low output impedance. Negative feedback through resistors sets a precise gain, so the same chip can buffer, invert, amplify or subtract signals.

For every configuration, Vout = G × Vin. Only the formula for the gain G changes. A real op-amp can't drive its output past its supply rails. These calculators assume it saturates at ±(VCC − 1.5 V) and warn you when the ideal answer goes beyond that.

    Filters

    A filter passes the frequencies you want and attenuates the rest. In instrumentation, filters remove noise such as mains hum or switching interference before a signal is measured or digitised.

    The cut-off frequency fc is where the output has fallen to 0.707 of the pass-band value, which is −3 dB. Passive filters use only R, L and C, so RC and RL passive filters can never make the signal bigger than the input. Active filters add an op-amp to provide gain and to stop the load from shifting the response. In these calculators Vin and Vout are sine-wave amplitudes. Use peak values for both, or RMS for both.

      Passive vs active filters

      PassiveActive
      ComponentsResistors, capacitors and inductors onlyOp-amps, transistors or FETs, plus resistors and capacitors. Usually no inductors.
      Power supplyNone neededNeeds a DC supply
      GainAt most 1 for RC and RL filters: the output is never larger than the inputCan be greater than 1, set accurately by the feedback resistors
      Effect of the loadThe load changes the filter's characteristicsThe op-amp buffers the output, so the load has little effect
      Frequency rangeWorks to very high frequencies, around 500 MHzLimited by the op-amp, often to about 500 kHz
      Size and practicalityInductors are bulky, dissipate power and create stray magnetic fieldsSmall, light and easy to integrate. Used for Butterworth, Chebyshev and Cauer designs.
      DrawbacksNo gain, and sensitive to loadingMore sensitive to component and environmental changes
